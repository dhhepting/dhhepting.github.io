Gouraud vs Phong shading

Both canvases draw the same triangle with the same light and the same lighting equation. The only difference is where the equation is evaluated: Gouraud (left) lights the three vertices and blends the three colours across the triangle; Phong (right) blends the surface normal and lights every pixel. The vertex normals tilt outward, as on a curved surface, so a specular highlight can fall in the middle of the triangle, where no vertex can see it.

How to read it

  • Above the centre. The light sits over the middle of the triangle. Phong shows a bright spot there. At the three vertices the highlight is nearly zero, so Gouraud has nothing bright to interpolate and misses the highlight completely.
  • Above a vertex. With the default placement the highlight lands exactly on that vertex. Both models show it there, because the vertex is where Gouraud evaluates the equation. Gouraud smears it into a linear fade toward the other two corners, so the centre reads about a third as bright (the average of the vertex values) even though the true highlight is nowhere near the centre; Phong keeps a tight spot. Switch the placement to “straight above” to see that a light directly over a vertex does not put the highlight on it, because the normal there is tilted.
  • Between the two. Drag the light slowly from a vertex toward the centre and watch the Gouraud highlight fade away while the Phong highlight travels with the light.
  • Curvature. With curvature 0 the surface is flat and the two pictures are much closer. The normals changing across the triangle is what Gouraud cannot represent.

Both shaders compute L, E and H from the eye-space position (not from interpolated vectors), so the left and right pictures differ only in where the equation runs.
